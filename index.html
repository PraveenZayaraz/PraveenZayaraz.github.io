Case study

AI Question Paper Generator

Writing a balanced exam paper by hand takes a teacher hours. This tool drafts one from the course's own syllabus and textbook, and shows where every question came from.

  1. Read the syllabusRules find units and topics. Llama 3 is the fallback, and any topic it invents is discarded.
  2. Index the textbookPages become overlapping passages with page numbers. Exercise lists and references are skipped.
  3. RetrieveFAISS vector search and BM25 keyword search are merged with reciprocal rank fusion.
  4. GenerateLlama 3 writes from one section of the book, at the Bloom's level the marks call for.
  5. CheckVague, duplicate or weakly grounded questions are rejected and retried.

Measured on a 962-page textbook

Passages indexed3,135
Syllabus topics found, with no manual mapping102
Topics matched to textbook material102 of 102
Questions generated in a 20-topic evaluation19 of 20
Mean grounding score against source passages0.67
Valid on the first attempt89.5%
Mean time per question, running locally3.8 s

Decisions that mattered

  • Rules before the model. A rule-based syllabus parser is instant and cannot invent topics, so the language model is only a fallback.
  • Two searches, fused. Embeddings blur exact terms such as CRC and IPv4. Keyword search catches them.
  • One section per question. Mixing passages from different chapters made questions drift, so sources must sit within a few pages of each other.
  • Llama 3 over Flan-T5. In my earlier comparison it scored 0.74 against 0.55 on relevance and ran faster.
  • A known limit. Grounding measures similarity, not correctness, so the output is a draft for a teacher to review.
